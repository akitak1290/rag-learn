Immigration and Customs in Vietnam
On arrival in Vietnam, you must complete white and blue duplicate arrival-departure forms. Usually, you’ll get these forms on
the plane on your flight to Vietnam. But, if not, you can find them in the immigration lounge at the airport.
Immigration will take the white copy; you will keep the blue copy with your passport at all times. Hotels, guest houses and
private hosts are required by law to register overnight guests with the local police. These blue forms are essential for this
purpose.
Incoming visitors must also fill out duplicate yellow and white customs declaration forms. The yellow copy is to remain with
your passport until departure. Don’t lose it!
Note:
Customs regulations are subject to change at any time. Therefore, the brief summary above is for reference only. RMIT
Vietnam cannot be held liable for any costs, damage, delays, or other detrimental events resulting from non-compliance.
Always double check with your local embassy or consulate.

Changing money
In Ho Chi Minh City, in the city centre and newly
developed areas (such as districts 1, 3, 7 (where RMIT’s
campus is located) and 10, prices are usually higher than
other places (such as districts 4, 6, Binh Thanh, Tan Phu
and Tan Binh).
In Hanoi, prices in districts close to the Old Quarter
(districts such as Hoan Kiem, Tay Ho, Hai Ba Trung, Ba
Dinh (where RMIT’s campus is located) and Dong Da)
tend to be higher than in other districts (such as Cau
Giay, Ba Dinh, Thanh Xuan and Hoang Mai).
The cost of living in both cities varies depending on your lifestyle. You can, for example, eat a bowl of soup on the street for
USD 1 or go for a European-style dinner with wine for USD 100.
The living standards in Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi can also vary depending on the area where you are located.

Points of interest
Vietnam is more than a country; it’s an experience! So don’t spend
all of your time with your head buried in textbooks!
Take the opportunity to explore and to check out the following
places when you arrive in Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi:
The Independence Palace is a not-to-be-missed destination in Ho Chi Minh City,
as it once served as the living and working quarters to French and Japanese
colonialists.
Ben Thanh Market is home to nearly 1,500 booths with more than 6,000 small
businesses selling wholesale and retail items. Each day, it welcomes more than
10,000 visitors to shop for goods at affordable prices.
Also known as “Backpacker Street”, the Pham Ngu Lao / Bui Vien Street boasts
Ho Chi Minh City’s signature vibrant nightlife, with a wide range of bars, pubs and
street food stands, making it the most beloved local and tourist hangout hotspot
in the city.
If you’re looking for a fun place to explore with many Instagram-worthy corners,
this is the place! The Cafe Apartments building is situated right in the heart of the
Nguyen Hue walking street, with many lovely tea shops, vintage cafes, eateries
and boutiques occupying the former apartment block.

The Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum is the top tourist attraction in Hanoi, as it honours
President Ho Chi Minh – the country’s national hero. The mausoleum is open for
a limited time during the day. You may have to queue before going inside.
St Joseph’s Cathedral is the most ‘checked-in’ place among both international
and local tourists. It was built in 1886 in a neo-gothic style. Its interior, with
coloured windows and religious paintings, remains untouched.
Hoan Kiem Lake is a major landmark where you can enjoy the green
surroundings and get away from the city’s noise and frenetic pace. A feature
of the lake is Ngoc Son Temple – another tourist attraction.
The first university of Vietnam, the Temple of Literature, is a well-preserved
historical building that showcases the traditional architecture of Vietnam. This is
also where Hanoian students often visit before exams to pray for good luck.

The Old Quarter is the essence of Hanoi’s culture. The 36 little streets in the
quarter are each named for a commodity once sold by businesses on those
streets during the period of French colonisation. It’s also a great area for trying
traditional food from the roadside stalls or for bargaining for souvenirs at
boutiques shops.
